How to Choose a Reliable Electrician in Glasgow: A Homeowner's Guide

14 July 2026 · 7 min read

Key Takeaways

  • Registration with NICEIC or SELECT is the single most important thing to check — it means the electrician is independently assessed and re-inspected
  • A registered contractor can self-certify work as meeting Building Regulations, saving you a separate council inspection
  • Genuine reviews and a real trade profile (Checkatrade, TrustATrader, MyBuilder) tell you more than a polished website
  • A free, no-obligation quote before any work starts should be standard, not a bonus

"Electrician" isn't a protected title in the UK the way "solicitor" or "doctor" is — legally, anyone can put it on a van. That's exactly why registration and verification matter more in this trade than almost any other, and why it's worth knowing what to actually check before you let someone into your consumer unit.

Check registration first, not last

The single most useful check is whether the electrician is registered with NICEIC or SELECT (Scotland's own electrotechnical trade association). Registration means their work has been independently assessed against national wiring standards and is re-inspected on an ongoing basis — it's not a one-off badge, it's active oversight.

Both bodies publish a searchable register on their websites, so you can verify a company's registration yourself before booking, rather than just taking a claim on trust.

Why "approved" actually matters

An approved or registered contractor can self-certify their own work as meeting Building Regulations. That's a genuine practical benefit, not just a certificate on the wall — it means a job like a rewire or a new circuit doesn't need a separate council building control inspection, which saves you time and arranging hassle you'd otherwise have to organise yourself.

Reviews and trade profiles over a polished website

A well-designed website tells you someone can build a website, not that they can wire a consumer unit safely. Genuine, verifiable reviews and a real profile on Checkatrade, TrustATrader, or MyBuilder are a better signal, because they're tied to actual completed jobs and can't be easily faked the way on-site copy can.

What a proper quote should look like

A free, no-obligation quote before any work starts should be the baseline expectation, not something to feel grateful for. It should be itemised clearly enough that you know what you're paying for materials versus labour, and it shouldn't require a deposit before anyone's even surveyed the job.

Red flags worth walking away from

A few things are worth treating as genuine warning signs rather than minor quirks.

  • Reluctance to confirm NICEIC or SELECT registration, or vague answers when asked directly
  • No fixed business address or landline — mobile-only contact with no other trace of the business
  • Pressure to pay a large deposit before any survey or written quote
  • No mention of testing or certification once work is finished
  • Cash-only insistence with no paperwork offered

Frequently Asked Questions

Is NICEIC or SELECT registration a legal requirement?
No, but it's the clearest way to verify competence and it allows the electrician to self-certify work as meeting Building Regulations, which an unregistered contractor can't do.
What's the difference between NICEIC and SELECT?
NICEIC operates UK-wide, while SELECT is Scotland's own electrotechnical trade association. Either is a genuine mark of registration — what matters is that the electrician holds one of them.
Should I get more than one quote?
It's sensible for larger jobs like a full rewire, but for smaller work a single quote from a registered, well-reviewed electrician is usually enough to move ahead confidently.
How can I check reviews are genuine?
Look for reviews spread across multiple platforms (Google, Checkatrade, TrustATrader) rather than only on the company's own website, and check they reference specific, plausible details rather than generic praise.
