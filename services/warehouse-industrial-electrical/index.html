Behind The Job

Planning Distribution Around How the Floor Actually Works

Warehouse and industrial electrical work tends to fail when it's planned around a generic layout rather than the specific site — machinery gets moved during a fit-out, pick stations shift, and a distribution plan that doesn't account for that ends up needing costly rework. We work from the actual intended floor plan and talk through likely changes before finalising a distribution layout.

Coordination with other trades matters just as much on these projects — warehouse fit-outs usually run several trades in parallel, and electrical work that isn't sequenced properly with racking, flooring, or fire systems installation causes delays for everyone. We plan our schedule around the wider project, not in isolation.

FAQs

Common Questions About Warehouse & Industrial Electrical Installations

Can you work to a tight fit-out deadline?
Yes, we regularly work to project deadlines alongside other trades — we'll be upfront if a timeline genuinely isn't realistic rather than agreeing and missing it.
Do you provide certification for the full installation?
Yes, full testing and certification is provided for the completed installation, covering the scope of electrical work carried out.
Do you handle full warehouse fit-outs or just specific jobs?
Both — from a full installation on an empty unit through to a specific addition like new machinery power on an already-operating site.
How do you plan power distribution across a large floor area?
We work from your actual floor layout and equipment positions, planning distribution runs and points based on how the space is genuinely used, not a generic grid.
Can you work around other trades on a live fit-out?
Yes, this is a common part of warehouse and industrial jobs — we coordinate scheduling with other trades and the site manager as standard.
Do you install power for specific machinery?
Yes, including assessing the machinery's specific power requirements and running a dedicated, correctly rated supply to it.
Can you quote for a large-scale installation without an initial site visit?
Not accurately — a site visit is standard for warehouse and industrial scale work, given how much the layout and existing infrastructure affects the quote.
