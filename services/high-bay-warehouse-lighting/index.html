Behind The Job

Why Fitting Count Alone Doesn't Determine Good Coverage

A common mistake with high-bay lighting upgrades is simply matching the number of existing fittings with new LED equivalents, without recalculating for the actual output and spread of the new fittings. LEDs distribute light differently to older fluorescent or sodium fittings, so a like-for-like fitting count can genuinely leave a warehouse under- or unevenly lit even after an upgrade.

We calculate layouts from ceiling height, floor area, and the lux level appropriate to how each zone is actually used — a picking aisle and a loading bay don't need identical lighting, and treating them the same either overspends on fittings or leaves working areas under-lit.

FAQs

Common Questions About High-Bay & Warehouse Lighting

Will the warehouse need to shut down during the upgrade?
Not usually — phased or zoned upgrade work can often be carried out around ongoing operations, and we'll plan this with you specifically for your site.
Do LED high-bay fittings need much maintenance?
Considerably less than fluorescent or sodium fittings — longer fitting lifespan is one of the main practical benefits alongside the energy savings.
How much can LED high-bay lighting actually save on running costs?
It varies by site and current fittings, but replacing older fluorescent or sodium high-bay lighting with LED commonly cuts lighting energy use significantly, on top of far longer fitting lifespan and less maintenance.
How do you work out how many fittings a warehouse needs?
We calculate based on ceiling height, floor area, and required lux levels for the space's use, rather than a generic fittings-per-square-metre estimate.
Can you add motion sensors to reduce running costs further?
Yes, zoned motion-sensor control is a common addition for warehouse lighting, particularly in less frequently used areas like storage aisles.
Do you work at height safely on a live warehouse floor?
Yes, we follow proper working-at-height procedures and can coordinate access equipment and scheduling around your ongoing operations.
Can lighting be upgraded in phases rather than all at once?
Yes, a phased upgrade across sections of a warehouse is a common and practical approach, particularly for larger sites.
